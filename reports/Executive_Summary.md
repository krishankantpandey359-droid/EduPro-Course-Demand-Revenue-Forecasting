# EduPro — Executive Summary

## Objective

Analyse recorded course demand and revenue from the supplied EduPro tables, then evaluate monthly forecasts using chronological validation. Results are exploratory and are not a guarantee of future performance.

## Dataset

The bundled dataset contains four source tables:

- Users: 3,000 rows.
- Teachers: 60 rows.
- Courses: 60 rows.
- Transactions: 10,000 rows.

Transaction dates range from 1 January 2025 to 30 December 2025. The forecasting target definitions follow the project code:

- EnrollmentCount: distinct TransactionID count per course-month. This is a transaction-event count, not a verified unique-student count.
- CourseRevenue: sum of Transactions.Amount per course-month. The currency and treatment of refunds or retries are not independently verified.

## Chronological Forecast Backtest

The reproducible backtest trains through September 2025 and tests October–December 2025. It uses a rolling one-step-ahead design and only lagged transaction history, with the naive previous-month value as a baseline.

There are 360 course-month training rows and 180 course-month test rows per target after the lag-history requirements.

| Target | Best model | MAE | RMSE | WAPE | Naive lag-1 MAE | Naive lag-1 RMSE | Naive lag-1 WAPE |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| EnrollmentCount | Random Forest | 2.93 | 3.84 | 21.56% | 3.99 | 5.08 | 29.32% |
| CourseRevenue | Random Forest | 282.92 | 684.26 | 22.41% | 395.11 | 991.33 | 31.30% |

Random Forest outperformed the naive previous-month baseline on all three metrics for both targets in this specific holdout. This is a positive result for the October–December 2025 window, but a single year of source history and one three-month holdout do not establish stable performance across other years or seasons.

The results are generated by `scripts/run_chronological_backtest.py`. The script writes CSV and JSON reports. The JSON records source-file SHA-256 hashes, data window, sample sizes, model metrics and baseline metrics. GitHub Actions runs the same script and uploads the reports as the `edupro-chronological-backtest` artifact.

## Historical Model Comparison

The older `outputs/model_comparison.csv` file contains a separate course-level cross-validation experiment with different targets and a different unit of analysis. Its results are not directly comparable to the current transaction-level monthly backtest and should not be quoted as current chronological forecast scores.

## Data Integrity and Interpretation

- No external dataset or fabricated rows are added to the bundled project data.
- Source rows are preserved; derived aggregates are calculated by the pipeline.
- Data-quality checks flag missing columns, unmatched identifiers, invalid dates, missing amounts and duplicate transaction IDs.
- Automated checks do not independently verify business definitions or the accuracy of every original source value.
- Repeated transactions need business review to determine whether they represent separate enrollments, retries, refunds or other events.

## Limitations

- The evaluation covers only October–December 2025.
- The source contains one calendar year, so multi-year seasonal behaviour cannot be assessed.
- The rolling one-step-ahead design assumes each preceding month is observed before the next forecast.
- EnrollmentCount is based on transaction IDs, not verified unique students or paid/completed enrolments.
- CourseRevenue uses the Amount field; the currency and refund/retry semantics are not confirmed.
- More years of data and a later untouched evaluation period are needed before operational use.

## Recommendations

1. Confirm the business meaning of TransactionID, repeated user-course purchases, refunds and retries.
2. Confirm the currency and accounting treatment of Transactions.Amount.
3. Add more historical years and evaluate an additional untouched holdout.
4. Monitor forecast errors by month and by course, not only in aggregate.
5. Treat model metrics as exploratory evidence, not proof of causation or a guarantee of future results.

## Conclusion

The current chronological backtest shows that Random Forest improves on a naive previous-month baseline for both recorded transaction-event counts and revenue in the October–December 2025 holdout. The result is promising but limited. Business definitions, source-data meaning and additional historical validation remain necessary before the forecasts guide operational decisions.
